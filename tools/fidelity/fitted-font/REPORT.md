# Fitted document font: measured results

Measured 2026-11-12 on Word 16.0.20527, through Word's own PDF export.
Tables for every document are regenerated by `report_tables.py` into
`out/tables.md`; the raw numbers are in `out/corpus_line/results.json`
and `out/corpus_word/results.json`.

## Verdict

The fitted font reaches the original 1/40 pt (0.025 pt) goal. On all 13
corpus documents, all 22,906 visible glyphs landed within 0.0066 pt of
the source (mean 0.0020 pt in line mode, 0.0024 pt in word mode). The
earlier recipe measured 0.063 to 0.102 pt on the same corpus. What is
left is the font unit itself: half a unit is 0.005 pt per axis.

## How it works

Each converted document carries its own embedded fonts, built from the
outlines found in the source PDF. Every word (or line) stays as real
typed characters. A substitution rule in the font replaces those
characters with one composite glyph that holds the exact glyph
placements. Word snaps the composite's origin to its 1/600 inch grid;
one checking export reads where each origin landed, and the leftover
error is moved into the composite's outlines. Baking never changed the
layout: 0 of 5,193 pieces moved between the two exports.

## Editing in Word

One word was replaced through Word in every document. Every glyph on
every other line stayed unchanged to 1/10000 pt, in 13 of 13 documents.

- Line mode (one glyph per run of a line): the whole edited line falls
  back to ordinary glyphs, and words before the edit move 0.16 to 9.3 pt.
- Word mode (one glyph per word and its trailing spaces): words before
  the edit keep their exact glyph; the new word and the words after it
  are ordinary glyphs shifted by the change in width.

Known flaw in word mode: after an edit, a later word can be drawn with
another occurrence's glyph of the same word (shape right, spacing off by
under 0.1 pt), because a run's first rule has no context. Fix before
porting: give each run's first piece a lookahead or its own slot.

## What Word allows (probes)

- With no `w14` run property Word applies no rule. `w14:ligatures`
  switches on `liga`; each `w14:stylisticSets` id switches on its own
  set. One font therefore gives 21 independent slots per run; a copy of
  the font under another name gives 21 more.
- One rule may cover up to 300 characters (longest tried), across spaces
  and across runs that differ only in `w:lang w:eastAsia`. `w:spacing`
  on a run stops rules from applying.
- Word's export fails on a composite that contains an ink-less glyph
  (a space) and on a glyph whose ink reaches 32.7 em from its origin.
  Pieces are cut at 31.5 em and spaces are left out of composites.
- A rule containing a character outside the basic plane never applies.
- Embedding: fonts with embedding permission 0 or 8 are used and
  re-embedded by Word. Permission 2 (restricted) is ignored and
  substituted. Permission 4 makes Word wait on a prompt under automation.

## What it does not fix

- The two documents that failed on fonts still do: one uses standard
  fonts that are not embedded (there is nothing to extract), the other
  forbids embedding.
- All of Word's export paths gave identical output, so there is no
  cheaper path around the grid. Text inside text boxes stays on the grid.

## Not verified

Other Word builds or machines, Word on screen or on paper (only its PDF
was measured), PDF readers other than pdfium, edits beyond one replaced
word, and more than two font copies.

## Adopting it in core/exact

1. Keep frames, baselines, grid fonts, pictures and shapes. Drop
   per-character runs and `w:spacing`.
2. Use word pieces. Each piece is its own run with alternating
   `w:lang w:eastAsia`.
3. Per grid font, add one composite per piece with exact offsets and a
   whole-pixel advance, and one chaining lookup per feature in which
   each piece backtracks on the previous piece's glyph.
4. Assign slots so no two runs in a slot start with the same piece.
5. Plan, make the one checking export, read each piece's origin by its
   outline, rewrite the composites.
6. Port the behaviours in `test_fitted_font.py` as unit tests.
