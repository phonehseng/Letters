# Slides fidelity report

How close Letters' own slide renderer is to PowerPoint and LibreOffice, slide
by slide, and how close each application is to the other on the same deck
(the baseline to beat). Generated by `tests/fidelity.rs`; the tables between
the markers are rewritten on every run, the analysis below them by hand.

Measures, per slide (every PDF read by the same pdfium reader):

- **lines agree**: share of the reference's text lines that the candidate
  breaks at the same characters (spaces ignored)
- **baseline Δ / left Δ**: mean distance of those lines' baselines and starts
- **box Δ**: for each shape or picture box of the reference, the mean edge
  distance to the nearest box of the candidate (capped at 50 pt)
- **SSIM**: structural similarity of the pages rendered 640 pixels wide

## Analysis (by hand)

Corpus: 12 decks built through PowerPoint's own automation
(`tests/fidelity/powerpoint_corpus.ps1`), 16 slides, exported to PDF by
PowerPoint 16 and by LibreOffice 26.8 (headless, private profile) on this
machine; LibreOffice's ODP saves of the same decks as its own decks. Fonts: the
decks use Aptos and Aptos Display (installed); no substitutions were needed.

### Against PowerPoint

Letters breaks every line of every slide where PowerPoint does (100 %;
LibreOffice 51.2 %), with a mean baseline difference of 0.30 pt (LibreOffice
3.71 pt), and is closer to PowerPoint than LibreOffice is on 12 of 16 slides by
SSIM (mean 0.972 vs 0.937).

Ranked causes of the remaining differences:

1. **Line pitch and baseline (fixed, the biggest).** The first run broke lines
   correctly but baselines were 11.5 pt off. Measured on pp_title_bullets:
   PowerPoint's line pitch is 1.2 x size x spacing (24 pt at 90 % gives 25.9 pt,
   whatever the font's own metrics say), and the baseline sits at the font's hhea
   ascent share of the line (0.75 for Aptos/Calibri). With those two rules every
   baseline of that deck is within 0.1 pt and the corpus mean fell to 0.30 pt
   (`layout/lines.rs`, `layout/mod.rs` cite this). Space before the first
   paragraph of a box is not added, as PowerPoint does.
2. **Textured theme backgrounds (pp_theme_ion, SSIM 0.97 vs LibreOffice 0.98).**
   Ion's background is a picture recoloured with `a:duotone`; without a JPEG
   decoder the picture is drawn as the mix of its two colours, so the tone is
   right but the texture is missing. A decoder plus the duotone mapping would
   close it.
3. **Shape and connector strokes (pp_shapes, pp_groups_connectors, SSIM 0.993
   vs 0.997; box differences 2.3-2.5 pt).** Not measured in detail yet. Strokes
   are drawn centred with miter joins and without arrowheads or effects, so the
   likely causes are join and cap details and the connector's elbow position.
4. **Pictures (box difference 7.5 pt for both Letters and LibreOffice)** is most likely the
   reader's box of the rotated picture (not checked); the rendered pixels match (SSIM 1.000).
5. **Tables (box difference 19 pt, SSIM 0.981 vs LibreOffice 0.968).** Text and
   fills match PowerPoint (baselines 0.09 pt); the default table style's white
   grid is drawn as lines while PowerPoint's PDF has differently shaped grid
   objects, which the box measure counts as far apart.
6. **pp_autofit_notes (SSIM 0.79, both applications low).** The text overflows
   the slide in PowerPoint's own PDF (PowerPoint did not recompute the stored
   autofit when the deck was made by automation); Letters shows the stored
   state, as PowerPoint does. The SSIM is low for both renderers because of the
   overlapping text, not because lines differ (100 %, 0.45 pt).

### Against LibreOffice (its own ODP decks)

Letters (reading the ODP) is about as close to LibreOffice as PowerPoint is
(mean SSIM 0.890 vs 0.895, closer on 6 of 10 slides), but agrees on few lines
(16 % vs PowerPoint's 19 %): Letters lays text out by PowerPoint's rules, while
LibreOffice uses the fonts' own line heights for proportional spacing and works
out shrink-on-overflow itself. A LibreOffice flavour of the box layout (as
core/layout has for documents) is the fix; it is not done.

### Not drawn yet

Charts and SmartArt (kept in the file, not rendered; PowerPoint stores no
picture for them), effects (shadows, glow, soft edges, 3D), picture recolouring,
pattern fills (drawn as a half-strength colour), arrowheads, vertical and
rotated text inside shapes, and custom shapes whose ODP paths use formulas. Each
is reported in the render report when met.
